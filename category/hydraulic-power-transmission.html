Technical & Buyer's Guide

Understanding Hydraulic & Power Transmission

Learn how to select the right hydraulic and gearbox lubricants to achieve smooth power transmission, prevent cavitation, and extend component life.

Key Selection Parameters

To keep high-pressure systems and heavy gears running smoothly, consider these mechanical factors:

  • 1
    Viscosity & Viscosity Index (VI) The oil must be thick enough to prevent leakage at high temperatures, but fluid enough to flow at startup. High VI oils maintain stable viscosity across a wide temperature spectrum.
  • 2
    Anti-Wear (AW) & Extreme Pressure (EP) Additives AW additives (like ZDDP) protect piston, vane, and gear pumps from wear under high pressure. EP additives are crucial for gearboxes under heavy shock loads to prevent gear tooth scoring.
  • 3
    Water Demulsibility The ability of the oil to separate from water contamination. Good demulsibility allows water to settle to the bottom of the reservoir so it can be drained, preventing rust and chemical breakdown.
  • 4
    Air Release & Foam Control Fluids must release entrained air bubbles quickly. Trapped air causes "cavitation" (violent collapse of micro-bubbles), which creates loud noise, destroys pump metal, and makes system response sluggish.

Why it Matters

Hydraulic fluid is not just a lubricant—it is the actual medium that transfers force in jacks, presses, molding machines, and heavy machinery. Using the wrong fluid or neglecting oil quality leads to severe internal wear, internal leakage, elevated operating temperatures, and sluggish cycle times.

Maintenance Tip

Fluid cleanliness is the single most critical factor in hydraulic lifespan. Implement regular filtration cycles and check ISO cleanliness levels. Over 70% of hydraulic component failures are caused by microscopic solid contaminants in the fluid.
