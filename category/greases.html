Specialty Lubricants

Application-specific oils, greases, and protective compounds for demanding plant conditions where standard lubricants are not enough.

Specialty lubricant selection depends on exposure to corrosion, food-safety requirements, temperature, electrical insulation, load, and relubrication practice.

Rust Protection Oils

Protective oils and films for machined parts, tools, components, and stored metal surfaces exposed to humidity or handling.

Food Grade & Silicone Lubricants

Specialty lubricants for food-processing equipment, rubber and plastic compatibility, clean operation, and non-staining applications.

Anti-Seize & Dielectric Greases

Compounds for threaded assemblies, electrical contacts, insulating applications, and parts exposed to heat, moisture, or seizure risk.

Specialty Industrial Greases

High-performance greases for heavy load, water exposure, high temperature, slow speed, and difficult industrial bearing applications.

Key Selection Parameters

Choosing the correct specialty lubricant requires matching the product chemistry to the risk you are trying to control:

  • 1
    Application Exposure Identify whether the main challenge is rust, washdown, food-zone contact, high temperature, electrical insulation, or heavy load before choosing the lubricant family.
  • 2
    Compatibility Requirements Food-grade and silicone lubricants must be compatible with seals, plastics, rubber, and process requirements. Electrical applications need products that preserve insulation performance.
  • 3
    Protection Duration Rust preventive oils and protective films should be selected by indoor or outdoor storage, handling frequency, required film type, and expected protection period.
  • 4
    Load, Temperature & Additives Anti-seize compounds and industrial greases need suitable solid lubricants, EP additives, and thickener systems for heat, shock load, water exposure, and long relubrication intervals.

Why it Matters

Specialty lubricants solve problems that standard oils cannot: corrosion during storage, seizure on threaded assemblies, clean lubrication in food plants, electrical insulation, and high-load grease protection in difficult environments.

Maintenance Tip

Do not interchange specialty products without checking compatibility. Food-grade approvals, dielectric properties, rust preventive film type, and grease thickener chemistry can all change performance in service.
