Industrial Oils & Lubricants

Greases

Semi-solid lubricants that stay in place, ideal for bearings, joints, and parts that cannot use liquid oil.

Grease selection depends on load, temperature, speed, water exposure, thickener type, and relubrication practice.

High Temperature Greases

Formulated with synthetic base fluids and special thickeners (like polyurea, bentonite, or PFPE) to withstand extreme thermal environments.

Lithium/Lithium Complex Greases

The industry standard for multi-purpose bearing lubrication, offering good water resistance and thermal stability.

Calcium/Calcium Sulfonate Greases

Provides outstanding water resistance and high load-carrying capacity, ideal for wet, humid, and corrosive environments.

Polyurea Greases

Premium high-speed grease widely used in electric motor bearings, offering excellent oxidation resistance and long service life.

Bentonite/Clay-based Greases

Non-melting greases designed for high-temperature applications where standard soap-thickened greases would melt and run out.

Aluminium Complex Greases

Excellent water resistance and mechanical shear stability, commonly used in food-grade and steel mill bearings.

Moly Greases

Fortified with molybdenum disulfide (moly) to provide solid dry lubrication film protection under extreme shock load conditions.

Multi-Purpose EP Greases

General-purpose extreme-pressure greases for heavy industrial gears, couplings, and sliding surfaces.

Wheel Bearing Greases

Designed to resist high shear, heat, and water washout in automotive and heavy equipment wheel hubs.

Open Gear Greases

Highly adhesive, tacky compounds designed to protect large open gears and wire ropes from friction and wear.

Technical & Buyer's Guide

Understanding Industrial Greases

Learn how to select the right grease consistency, thickener, and base oil to protect heavily loaded bearings and joints from failure.

Key Selection Parameters

Choosing the correct industrial grease requires analyzing the environment, load, speed, and temperature profile:

  • 1
    Base Oil Viscosity The core lubricant inside grease is oil. High viscosity base oil is needed for heavy loads and slow speeds, while light viscosity oil is used for high-speed precision bearings.
  • 2
    Thickener Type The "sponge" that holds the oil. Lithium, lithium complex, calcium, polyurea, and bentonite clay thickeners determine the grease's heat limit, water resistance, and structural stability.
  • 3
    NLGI Consistency Grade Measured from 000 (semi-fluid, like cooking oil) to 6 (very hard, like block grease). NLGI Grade 2 is the standard for most electric motor and rolling element bearings.
  • 4
    Extreme Pressure (EP) & Solid Additives EP additives (like sulfur-phosphorus) or solid lubricants (like molybdenum disulfide) prevent metal-to-metal welding under heavy loads and high vibrations.

Why it Matters

Unlike fluid oils, grease stays in place, forming a physical seal that blocks moisture, dust, and contaminants from entering moving assemblies. Selecting the wrong grease can cause "grease washout" in wet environments, thermal breakdown under heat, or dry friction wear due to oil starvation.

Maintenance Tip

Never mix different grease types without checking compatibility charts. Incompatible thickeners (like lithium and polyurea) can react chemically, causing the grease to soften, separate, and completely run out of the bearing.
