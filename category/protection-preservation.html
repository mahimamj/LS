Technical & Buyer's Guide

Understanding Protection & Preservation

Learn the factors involved in preventing metal oxidation during storage/transit and ensuring clean release in molding operations.

Key Selection Parameters

Corrosion protection and mould release require selecting fluids based on exposure conditions and post-process needs:

  • 1
    Storage and Transit Environment Evaluate if components will face dry indoor storage, humid sea transit, or direct outdoor weathering. Harsh outdoor exposure requires heavier, more resilient waxy films.
  • 2
    Film Type and Thickness Select between solvent-based (leaves dry, touch-safe, or waxy film), oil-based (leaves a thin lubricative film), and water-dispersible (non-flammable, eco-friendly) rust preventives.
  • 3
    Ease of Film Removal Before parts are painted, welded, or assembled, protective films must be removed. Choose a formulation that easily washes off with standard solvent degreasers or hot alkaline washes.
  • 4
    Mould Release Temperature and Spread Mould release oils must spread uniformly and withstand the heat of concrete casting or plastic molding. This prevents sticking, staining, and surface defects on the molded product.

Why it Matters

Unprotected metal begins to oxidize immediately in contact with moisture and air, turning finished parts into scrap. Rust preventives form an impermeable barrier that seals out atmospheric humidity. In molding, a high-quality release agent speeds up production cycles and avoids damage to both the mould and the finished part.

Maintenance Tip

Always apply rust preventives to completely clean, dry metal surfaces. If fingerprints, cutting coolant residue, or water droplets are left on the metal before spraying, they will be trapped under the protective oil barrier and still cause rust.
